% Description of the adapter board and its single-ended to differential front end

\newcommand{\FrontEndDescription}{%
The chip sits on the adapter board \texttt{IHP130\_ASICONE\_ADP\_QFN48\_0p5Q\_Adapter}, which
plugs into the ULX3S header and carries the supplies, the references and the front end of the ADC.

\paragraph{Supplies.} The \SI{5}{V} of the ULX3S header feeds two LDOs: a TLV75533 (\SI{3.3}{V}),
which supplies the op-amps and the I/O of the chip, and a TLV75512 (\SI{1.2}{V})~\cite{tlv755p},
which supplies the core of the chip and, through jumper JP5, the supply pin of the ADC
(\texttt{adc\_vdd}, pin~2). The ADC therefore runs at \SI{1.2}{V}. Every supply pin of the chip has
\SI{10}{\micro F} and \SI{100}{nF} on the board. The \SI{1.2}{V} LDO is also the reference of the
three potentiometers and of the \SI{0.6}{V} divider of the front end.

\paragraph{Single-ended to differential converter.} Figure~\ref{fig:frontend} is the circuit of
the TI application note SBOA216~\cite{sboa216}, built with the two halves of an
LMV358~\cite{lmv358} on \SI{3.3}{V}. U5A is a unity-gain buffer and gives $v_p = v_{in}$; U5B is a
difference amplifier with two \SI{1}{k\ohm} resistors whose non-inverting input is the
\SI{0.6}{V} of the divider R1/R2, and gives $v_n = 2 \cdot \SI{0.6}{V} - v_{in}$. The ADC then sees
$v_d = v_p - v_n = 2 (v_{in} - \SI{0.6}{V})$ with a common mode of \SI{0.6}{V}. Each output goes
through \SI{47.5}{\ohm} to a capacitor network: \SI{1.2}{nF} across the pair (differential pole at
$1/(2\pi \cdot \SI{47.5}{\ohm} \cdot \SI{2.52}{nF}) = \SI{1.33}{MHz}$) and \SI{120}{pF} from each
side to ground (common-mode pole at \SI{28}{MHz}). This network is the charge reservoir for the
sampling capacitors of the ADC (about \SI{1}{pF} per side, 2400 times smaller), and the resistors
isolate the op-amps from the capacitive load. The DDS output drives the non-inverting input of
U5A, with jumper JP8 open so that the potentiometer RV3 is disconnected; the scope channel
measures the same node.

\paragraph{References.} RV1 and RV2 (\SI{10}{k\ohm} potentiometers on \SI{1.2}{V}) set the
references, buffered by the two halves of a second LMV358 (U4) and connected through jumpers JP6
and JP7: $V_{REFP} = \SI{\BoardVrefp}{V}$ (pin~45) and $V_{REFN} = \SI{\BoardVrefn}{V}$ (pin~44),
measured with a multimeter. There is no capacitor on the reference nets at the chip: the
op-amp outputs, the traces and the jumpers are all the ADC sees.

\paragraph{Ideal transfer.} With these values the differential LSB is
$2 (V_{REFP} - V_{REFN}) / 2^{\AdcBits} = \SI{25}{mV}$, that is \SI{\FeLsbIdeal}{mV} at the input of
the front end, and the transitions are ideally at
$T_k = \SI{\BoardVmid}{V} + (k - 2^{\AdcBits - 1}) \cdot \SI{\FeLsbIdeal}{mV}$: from
\SI{\FeTFirstIdeal}{V} to \SI{\FeTLastIdeal}{V}. In this report all voltages are single-ended, measured at the input of
the front end, so the gain and the common mode of the front end are part of the transfer characteristic.

\subsection{Signal chain}
Figure~\ref{fig:bench} shows the bench. The MSO20 generates the stimulus with its DDS and
measures the same node with its oscilloscope channel~1, which is the voltage reference of all
the measurements. The front end converts it to a differential signal for the ADC. The ADC is
clocked and read by a RISC-V system (RISCVConsole) running on a ULX3S FPGA board, which stores
the samples in RAM; the host reads them back through JTAG.

\subsection{ADC interface}
The ADC connects to the ULX3S header through the ADCFIFO peripheral of RISCVConsole
(base address \texttt{0x64003000}):
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\toprule
Signal & Direction & ULX3S pin & Function \\
\midrule
\texttt{valid} & FPGA $\to$ ADC & GN27 (E13) & Enables the conversions (control bit 0) \\
\texttt{rst\_n} & FPGA $\to$ ADC & GN26 (C13) & ADC reset: system reset or control bit 8 \\
\texttt{clk} & FPGA $\to$ ADC & GN25 (E14) & \SI{10}{MHz} conversion clock \\
\texttt{io\_valid} & ADC $\to$ FPGA & GN24 (D16) & A new result is ready \\
\texttt{bits[4:0]} & ADC $\to$ FPGA & GN23 \ldots\ GN19 & Result, MSB on GN23, LSB on GN19 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The results are captured on the ADC clock (control bit 16 selects the edge; the inverted edge is
used here) into an asynchronous FIFO of 8 entries that crosses into the \SI{50}{MHz} bus clock.
The firmware (\texttt{RISCVConsoleCode/src/adc.c}) enables the ADC, flushes the FIFO, then reads
the data register in a tight loop, storing \AdcSamples{} consecutive results in RAM. Bit 31 of the
data register flags an empty FIFO; the FIFO drops results when it is full, so the firmware also
counts the empty reads. In every capture of this report the empty reads are about a quarter of
all the reads, so the CPU outpaces the ADC and the record has no gaps. The capture duration
measured with the CLINT timer confirms the sample rate.

\subsection{Procedure}
Each measurement (\texttt{adc\_run.sh}) configures and enables the DDS, captures the scope, boots
the capture firmware through JTAG (\texttt{jtag-boot.sh}), logs its UART report, and dumps
\texttt{adc\_buf} through JTAG while the DDS keeps running. \texttt{adc\_sweep.sh} runs a ramp
and then the sine at every frequency of the sweep; \texttt{report/make\_report.py} analyzes the
runs and builds this document.
